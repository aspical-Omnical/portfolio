MINT

A mark made the way gold is tested, for my AI translation engine.

Gold is tested on a stone. A streak is rubbed on a touchstone, a drop of acid goes on it, and only real gold stays. The mark is that test, with the word left standing.

It exists twice, once in each language.

MINT is the AI translation engine I founded for Hebrew and English auction catalogues, with fact and number checks on every lot. Development began in December 2025.

MINT at work

A house brings its Hebrew catalogue. MINT translates every lot, checks each translation against what the house wrote, and brings a person only the lots that need a decision. Here is one decision, start to finish, in MINT’s public sandbox, on a sample lot.

BINA

One mark that reads in two languages, and the product family it carries.

The Hebrew letter ב is carried in the counters of the Latin B. Nothing is drawn: letters, space, and one heel.

BINA is the back office I built and run for TheBidder, an antiques and Judaica auction house. The identity had to hold a Hebrew-first interface, English catalogues and four products.

One mark, five tiles

The house and its four products share the monogram. Each takes one colour and one voice.

  1. BINA

  2. Bonton

  3. Tory

  4. Picnic

  5. Mint

Three of the rooms

Each sibling has a room in BINA. Tory keeps the inventory, Sheep the shipping, Rafik the trading floor. These are the live rooms, on the house’s own lots; a buyer’s details are blurred.

When they speak together

Every sibling has its own material. When several answer one message, the mail is signed with one plate, its field running through each speaker’s material in the order they speak, first speaker on the right, as Hebrew reads. Two at a time, then more, up to eight.

Captain Caption

A subtitling brand whose initials stand up and become its hero.

The mark starts as two C’s lying side by side. Then they stand up: one becomes the head, the other the shoulders, and the caption line stays under the feet.

That is the shift, and the whole identity hangs on it: the letters for small sizes, the hero for large ones, and the turn between them as the brand’s one move.

The mailers

A run of mails for every sale, from the announcement to the last hours.

Every TheBidder sale gets its own sequence: the announcement, the days before, sale day, then the after-sale until it closes. BINA builds each mail from the catalogue, and a person approves every send. It also makes the sale’s poster: it picks lots from across the catalogue, cuts each object out of its photograph and packs them together.

2024, then 2026

The house’s mail for its end-of-year sale in December 2024, before my time. Then mine: the May 2026 sale on Bidspirit, and the first mail of sale 138 in November, built by BINA. Each opens whole on a click.
